Privacy policy

This policy describes personal data handled by the ZZveenus Discord bot and dashboard.

Last updated: November 7, 2026

Who operates ZZveenus

The controller is the ZZveenus project operator. For privacy questions or requests, contact the operator by joining the ZZveenus Support server and opening a ticket in #make-ticket. A data protection officer has not been appointed.

Information handled and why

When ZZveenus is in a server, it handles Discord server, channel, role, and member IDs and, where needed, names and membership details to run commands, apply configuration, moderate, and deliver alerts. Server settings may include Steam filters and discount thresholds; YouTube channel and video IDs; game-code alert settings; announcement destinations; vote-kick and moderation settings; blacklist state; and subscription code references and expiry times.

If a server owner enables the economy or leveling modules, the bot stores member IDs with wallet and bank balances, economy cooldown timestamps, XP, levels, and configured level-reward role IDs. This data is saved in a server-specific file in data2. These modules are off until the server owner enables them.

When sticky roles are enabled, the bot stores member IDs, role IDs, and timestamps to restore roles after a rejoin. Records expire after 90 days. To support a one-time new-server notice, the bot stores a joining server’s ID, name, join time, and notice-attempt time. It sends the operator a notice after about one hour and retains the attempt record to avoid sending duplicates; that history has no automatic expiry.

YouTube channel-name and RSS validator caches store channel IDs, display names, and feed validators such as ETags and Last-Modified values. These are operational cache data, not a message archive. Steam metadata is cached in memory. Top.gg vote verification receives a user ID when a vote is checked; a successful check creates a 24-hour vote pass. The bot also posts its server count to Top.gg.

Legal bases

Where data-protection law such as the GDPR applies, the operator processes data as needed to provide features requested by server administrators, to pursue legitimate interests in operating, securing, and supporting the bot and dashboard, and to comply with legal obligations where applicable. The applicable basis depends on the feature and context.

Messages and commands

The bot does not keep a general archive of server messages. Discord may deliver message content to the bot because its features use the Message Content intent. For leveling, it uses a qualifying message to award XP but does not save that message’s text. Steam filter checks operate on Steam deal information and server-configured keywords. `/filter test` compares the sentence supplied in that command and does not store the sentence as a record. The bot’s private owner commands process the owner’s command text in memory to perform the requested action.

Dashboard sign-in

Dashboard sign-in uses Discord OAuth scopes identify and guilds. The dashboard receives your Discord ID, username/display name, avatar details, and server list/permissions so it can show servers and check access. OAuth access and refresh tokens, session identifiers, CSRF state, and temporary rate-limit and server-list caches are kept in server memory. Sessions expire after eight hours of inactivity or when the bot process restarts. The dashboard uses an HttpOnly session cookie; it does not use advertising or analytics cookies.

Dashboard requests use the connecting network address for short-lived in-memory rate limiting. Rejected host requests may log the remote address and supplied host headers to the PebbleHost console, at most once per minute. PebbleHost and other infrastructure providers may also keep their own access and security logs under their terms.

Regular settings are available only when you own the server or have Manage Server/Administrator permission and the bot is installed. The bot operator’s separate dashboard view exposes basic server information and blacklist controls only. Blacklist changes are logged to the bot console with the operator ID and affected server name/ID.

Storage, recipients, and services

Persistent bot data is stored on the bot host in server-specific JSON files under data and data2, separate sticky-role files, a global vote-pass file, subscription-code records, and global new-server notice state. The local website is hosted through PebbleHost. The legal-page copy is also hosted by GitHub Pages; those providers may process visitor connection data under their own policies.

Discord provides bot, identity, and server-list services. Top.gg provides vote verification and receives the bot’s server count. Steam and YouTube/Google services provide public profile, sale, channel, and video information. Public feed providers supply game-code data. Requests are made when the corresponding feature needs them. Each provider handles its own service data under its own privacy policy and terms; information may be processed in countries where those providers or their infrastructure operate.

Retention and deletion

Server configuration, economy data, levels, role rewards, and code-redemption records remain on the host while needed to operate the bot. They do not all expire automatically. Sticky-role records expire after 90 days; dashboard sessions expire after eight hours of inactivity; vote passes last 24 hours, though an expired pass entry may remain in its file until that file is next saved. YouTube display-name cache entries normally refresh after 24 hours. New-server notice history currently has no automatic expiry.

A server owner can use `/request-deletion` to send a manual request; the command does not delete data. To complete removal, the operator must remove the server’s data/<server-id>.json, data2/<server-id>.json if present, and data/stickyroles/<server-id>.json if present, and remove any linked redeemed subscription-code record. The request is processed manually. For access, correction, restriction, objection, or erasure requests about your personal data, contact the operator through the support ticket and specify the data concerned. Some records may need to be retained where a legal obligation or claim requires it.

Your rights and complaints

Depending on applicable law and circumstances, you may ask to access or correct your data, request erasure or restriction, object to processing, and lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority. If you are in Estonia, you can contact the Estonian Data Protection Inspectorate. You can also contact the supervisory authority in your place of residence or work.

Security and changes

Reasonable technical measures are used to protect stored data, including access checks, rate limits, and restricted dashboard sessions. No online service can guarantee absolute security. This policy may change when the bot or dashboard changes; the date above identifies the latest revision.
